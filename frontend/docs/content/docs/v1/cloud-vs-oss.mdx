# Cloud vs OSS

Hatchet is an MIT licensed open source project. Self-hosting is the easiest to start experiencing Hatchet. You can use Docker or Kubernetes with our official [Helm charts](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet-charts) to deploy your own instance.

When your workload increases and demands low latency, high throughput with the added guarantees of infrastructure reliability and scalability, you can depend on Hatchet Cloud to give you all of that and some more. With our managed offering you are able to focus on your workflows and business logic by offloading all of the infrastructure maintenance to us. Hatchet Cloud is also the first to get security updates and vulnerabilities resolved. That is to say - for most teams, Hatchet Cloud is the fastest and most reliable way to run Hatchet in production.

A move between the two does not change your worker code. To migrate, you simply point workers and clients at a new endpoint. Many teams self-host for development and staging, then use Cloud for production.

## Why production is the hard part

The programming model is the same in both versions. However, when you self-host a production instance, you take on these tasks:

- **Run PostgreSQL under load.** Hatchet uses Postgres as its source of truth. Under high load, you must manage connections, tune vacuum, and add read replicas among other things. Under high stress, it is imperative that you monitor latency and API errors.
- **Tune the engine.** The Hatchet engine schedules and dispatches every task. Its throughput and latency depend on meaningful defaults but you must change these settings as your load grows.
- **Handle failures.** Failures such as node downtime, a full disk, network congestion, failed migrations are some of the reasons that may lead to service downtime.
- **Upgrade without downtime.** We have a 2 week release cadence. Some versions contain database migrations and security fixes that should land as soon as possible to avoid degradation of service.
- **Plan capacity.** In order to scale ahead of time, you must observe traffic and make meaningful tweaks to keep the service reliable.
